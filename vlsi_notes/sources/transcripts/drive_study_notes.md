### 2_Energy_Band_Lecture_Notes (typed, 10 pp)
Crystalline material (periodic in x, y, z; only crystals have a defined band gap). A full VB can't carry current (no empty states). Classification table: metal (no gap; Au, Ag, Zn), **insulator ~8 eV (Al₂O₃)**, semiconductor (Si 1.1, Ge 0.7; tunable). eV; kT = 0.0259 eV. Reciprocal space, k = 2π/λ, p = ħk, E = ħ²k²/2m (true only for a free electron in vacuum). Periodic potential → Schrödinger equation → distorted E–k with gaps at multiples of π/a. **First Brillouin zone: k from −π/a to +π/a**; near k = 0 approximately parabolic. m* = ħ²/(d²E/dk²); significance: condenses QM, lets F = ma be used, higher curvature → lower m* → higher mobility → higher current, high speed. Holes as quasi-particles; move with the field, +ve charge, in the VB; hole E–k diagram, m_h* ∝ 1/curvature. **Band structure (E–k) vs band diagram (E vs x).** Direct/indirect: direct has **high absorption coefficient**, efficient light emission; indirect needs phonons, lower absorption (Si solar cells still work). Fermi function; E_F a statistical construct, 50 % at any temperature.
**New vs v1:** ~8 eV insulator example, Brillouin zone, band structure vs band diagram, absorption coefficient.

### 3_Density_of_States_Equations_Images (typed, 5 pp; equations are images)
DOS definition (states per eV per cm³), CGS units. g_c(E) ∝ √(E − E_c) starting at E_c; g_v(E) ∝ √(E_v − E). n = ∫ g f dE. Finite n even in intrinsic Si (≈10¹⁰ cm⁻³). Simplified n = N_c F_{1/2}((E_F − E_c)/kT) with **Fermi–Dirac integral of order ½**. **N_c, N_v** (effective DOS) depend on m* and T. Intrinsic n = p = n_i.
Standard formulas: g_c(E) = (4π(2m_n*)^{3/2}/h³)√(E − E_c); N_c = 2(2π m_n* kT/h²)^{3/2}; N_v = 2(2π m_p* kT/h²)^{3/2}.

### 4_DOS and Carrier_Concentration (typed, 3 pp)
N = N_c F_{1/2}(η), P = N_v F_{1/2}(η′). **Boltzmann approximation valid when E_c − E_F ≥ 3kT (electrons) or E_F − E_v ≥ 3kT (holes)**: n = N_c exp(−(E_c − E_F)/kT), p = N_v exp(−(E_F − E_v)/kT). **n_i = √(N_c N_v) exp(−E_g/2kT)**; n_i is the minimum carrier concentration (thermal); Si n_i ≈ 1.5×10¹⁰.

### 5_Equlibrium carrier concentration (typed, 4 pp)
Intrinsic/extrinsic; donors P, As; acceptor B; E_F positions. n_i cannot be reduced; **Ge (0.68 eV) high n_i, GaN (3.4 eV) very low n_i**; higher n_i → **leakage current / noise**. Formula table (n, p, n_i, mass action). E_F is flat in equilibrium; a slope means current. Forbidden gap: g = 0, so n = 0 even where f = 0.5. Flat E_c/E_v means no field. **Non-uniform doping in equilibrium:** E_F stays flat, the bands bend, and the built-in field balances diffusion.
**New vs v1:** non-uniform doping built-in field, leakage implications.

### 5_Carrier concentration and incomplete ionization (typed image, 6 pp)
Charge neutrality P + N_D⁺ = n + N_A⁻; compensated-material quadratic (same as class). **n vs T (log n vs 1/T): three regions:**
1. **Freeze-out** (≈10–50 K): kT ≪ E_c − E_D, donors not ionised, n falls exponentially.
2. **Extrinsic** (includes room T): ~100 % ionised, n ≈ N_D, the device operating range.
3. **Intrinsic** (very high T, ~500–700 °C for Si): n_i ∝ e^{−E_G/kT} ≫ N_D, conductivity uncontrolled. Wide-gap GaN and SiC tolerate higher T.

**Degenerate semiconductors:** Maxwell–Boltzmann n = N_c exp((E_F − E_c)/kT) is valid only when E_c − E_F > 3kT (≈78 meV at 300 K). At high doping E_F approaches or passes into the CB (degenerate, E_F > E_c). **Joyce–Dixon:** (E_F − E_c)/kT = ln(n/N_c) + (1/√8)(n/N_c), a transcendental equation solved numerically. **Band-gap narrowing:** heavy doping distorts the lattice potential, E_G → E_G′ (Si 1.1 → ~1.0 eV), plus **band tails**. **Incomplete ionisation:** e.g. Mg in GaN (p-type) is only 1–5 % ionised (10¹⁷ Mg → 10¹⁵ holes), which hurts white LEDs. 100 % ionisation needs E_c − E_D < kT; 3–4 kT means partial. **N_D⁺/N_D = 1/(1 + 2 exp((E_F − E_D)/kT))** (factor 2 from spin; E_F = E_D gives 1/3, i.e. 33 %). **N_A⁻/N_A = 1/(1 + 4 exp((E_A − E_F)/kT))** (factor 4: spin plus heavy- and light-hole bands). Neutrality uses N_D⁺ and N_A⁻.

### 6_Carrier scattering and mobility (typed image, 6 pp)
Random thermal motion: zig-zag, no net current, the electron returns near its start. Scattering mechanisms: **phonons** (lattice vibration, stronger with T) and **ionised impurities** (Coulomb). Scattering is not a physical hit: a potential perturbs the motion. **Phonon modes: acoustic and optical, each transverse and longitudinal.** τ = mean time between collisions. Drift cycle (accelerate, gain energy, collide, lose energy, repeat). Velocity = thermal (random, no current) + drift (directional, only with a field; the field can be internal from band slope). v = (qτ/m*)F, μ = qτ/m*, cm²/V·s. μ is the slope of v vs F; μ ∝ 1/m* (holes slower); μ ∝ τ. Low field (v = μF) vs high field (velocity saturates, dv/dF = 0, mobility no longer matters; many transistors operate there). T-dependence matters for design (CMOS logic can fail).

### 7_Carrier transport and trap statistics (typed image, 7 pp)
J_drift,n = qμn n E; J_diff,n = qDn dn/dx. μ ∝ qτ/m. **Einstein relation derivation:** n-type, equilibrium, non-uniform doping (doping falls left→right so E_c − E_F grows). n(x) = N_c exp((E_F − E_c(x))/kT); field E = −(1/q) dE_c/dx; dn/dx = n(x)·(−(1/kT) dE_c/dx); set qDn dn/dx = qμn n E (magnitudes) ⇒ Dn/kT = μn/q ⇒ **Dn/μn = kT/q**. Significance: D ∝ μ, get one from the other, **valid generally** even though derived at equilibrium. Generation (VB→CB) and recombination (radiative = light, non-radiative = heat/phonons). **Traps (SRH):** physical defects (dislocations, missing lines of atoms, vacancies, dangling bonds, impurities, interstitials) appear as discrete states E_T in the gap; they matter in p–n junctions, BJTs, LEDs and solar cells. **Four processes:** r_a electron capture, r_b electron emission, r_c hole capture, r_d hole emission. Trap occupancy f(E_t) = 1/(1 + exp((E_t − E_F)/kT)); empty traps N_t(1 − f). **r_a = σn v_th n N_t (1 − f)** (σn = capture cross-section in cm², v_th = thermal velocity, σn·v_th = volume swept per second). **r_b = e_n N_t f** (e_n = emission constant). Optical generation G_L (cm⁻³s⁻¹): dn/dt = G_L − (r_a − r_b). Steady state: r_a − r_b = r_c − r_d. R = (pn − n_i²)/(τ(…)); long τ means small R; **τ ∝ 1/(σ v_th N_t)** (the notes write n; physically the trap density).

### 8_Continuity equation and PN Junction diode (typed image, 7 pp)
Drift and diffusion formulas; Einstein. **Optical generation:** photon absorbed if hν = hc/λ ≥ E_G, so **λ ≤ hc/E_G** (the cut-off wavelength). SRH U = (1/τ)(pn − n_i²)/(p + n + 2n_i); n-type low injection U ≈ (p − p0)/τ = Δp/τ. **Continuity equation:** ∂n/∂t = (1/q) dJn/dx + G_L − U (from flow in minus flow out over A·dx). **Steady state, no field:** Dn d²Δn/dx² + G_L − Δn/τ = 0. With G_L = 0: d²Δn/dx² = Δn/(Dn τ) = Δn/Ln², **Ln = √(Dn τ)** (average distance an excess electron diffuses before recombining); solution Δn(x) = A1 e^{x/Ln} + A2 e^{−x/Ln}. **Example 1: uniform illumination** (no gradient) ⇒ **Δn = G_L τ**. **Example 2: light switched off at t = 0** ⇒ dΔn/dt = −Δn/τ ⇒ **Δn(t) = G_L τ e^{−t/τ}**, which is how lifetime is measured. p–n junction intro: assumptions are moderate doping (no band tailing or Joyce–Dixon), full ionisation, equilibrium (no light, magnetic or electric fields). Formation: diffusion, recombination near the junction, immobile N_A⁻ and N_D⁺, **depletion approximation** (absolutely no mobile carriers), built-in field balances diffusion.

### 9_PN Junction diode and characteristics (typed image, 8 pp)
Applications: LEDs, BJTs, photodetectors, solar cells. Ideal diode J = J0(e^{qVa/kT} − 1). **Generation–recombination current** from traps in the depletion region: **I_GR ∝ q(n_i/2τ)W**; total = ideal + G-R. **Four quadrants:** Q1 forward (LED); Q2 unused; Q3 reverse, illuminated (photodetector); Q4 illuminated (solar cell). **LED:** forward bias slightly above turn-on (e.g. 2.2–2.3 V for 2 V); needs a direct gap (Si and Ge can't); radiative band-to-band recombination in the depletion region (trap-assisted is non-radiative); **λ = hc/E_G ≈ 1242/E_G nm** (E_G in eV). Poor efficiency of simple junctions: carriers cross too fast, and the surrounding p/n regions **reabsorb** photons; fix with wider-gap p and n regions. **Photodetector:** reverse bias (Q3) or zero bias; E_photon ≥ E_G creates an EHP in the depletion region, and the field sweeps the e⁻ to n, the hole to p. **Dark current** (I_s + generation), **photocurrent**, sensitivity = photo/dark ratio, **avalanche photodetector (APD)** near breakdown. **Solar cell:** Q4, acts as a battery; same mechanism; **short-circuit current I_SC** at V = 0 (= photocurrent); self-biased with a load; efficiency from max power (**fill factor**); solar spectrum peaks around 500–550 nm, so choose material to match. **Breakdown** only in reverse (the whole field drops across depletion); destructive only if current is not limited. **Zener (tunnelling):** both sides heavily doped, narrow W; VB electrons on p tunnel to CB states on n through a thin triangular barrier; **V_BR ≈ 4E_G/q (≈4.4 V for Si)**. **Avalanche (impact ionisation):** most common; less doping; chain reaction; **V_BR > 6E_G/q (> 6.6 V Si)**. Impact-ionisation coefficient **α_n = (1/n)(Δn/Δx)**; **multiplication factor M = 1/(1 − ∫₀^W α(x)dx)**, breakdown when the integral → 1 (M → ∞).
(9, continued) **Critical field** F_crit: Si ≈ 400 kV/cm, GaN ≈ 3 MV/cm, weakly dependent on doping. **V_BR = ½·W·F_max** (area of the triangular field). Wide gap blocks more: for W = 1 μm, GaN 180 V vs Si 20 V (high-power devices). **C–V profiling:** junction/depletion capacitance C = Aε0εs/W; reverse bias ↑ ⇒ W ↑ ⇒ C ↓; C = ΔQ_D/ΔV_a. **One-sided abrupt (p⁺n) junction:** **1/C² = 2(V_bi − V_A)/(qε0εs N_D)** (per unit area, so A² in the denominator if C is total): a straight line. **|slope| = 2/(qε0εs N_D) gives N_D** (lightly doped side); the **intercept at 1/C² = 0 gives V_bi** (V_A = V_bi).

### 10_PN Junction diode under equilibrium (typed image, 10 pp)
Static condition: no voltage, no light, no current. Depletion region formation; **neutral regions have no field**. Drift = diffusion, total = 0. **E_F must be constant**; aligning the separate Fermi levels bends the bands. Slope in E_c/E_v only in depletion. **"Cemented floor" picture:** an electron rolls downhill on E_c toward n, so the field points n → p; it pushes diffusing electrons back to n and holes back to p, acting as a barrier. **V_bi** = difference between the E_c's far from the junction; extremely important; barrier height; **cannot be measured directly**; sets W.
**Derivation:** φ_n = E_c − E_F (n-side) = (kT/q) ln(N_c/N_D); φ_p = E_F − E_v (p-side) = (kT/q) ln(N_v/N_A). **qV_bi = E_G − φ_p − φ_n** = E_G − (kT/q) ln(N_c N_v/(N_A N_D)). With n_i² = N_c N_v e^{−E_G/kT}: ln(N_c N_v) = ln n_i² + E_G/kT ⇒ the E_G terms cancel ⇒ **V_bi = (kT/q) ln(N_A N_D/n_i²)**. Dependence: more doping gives larger V_bi; wider gap (lower n_i) gives larger V_bi (GaN > Si); **maximum V_bi ≈ E_G/q** at degenerate doping.
**Depletion width:** W = W_n + W_p; **N_A W_p = N_D W_n**; W_p/W_n = N_D/N_A; p⁺n with N_A = 1000 N_D gives W ≈ W_n. **Poisson:** d²V/dx² = −ρ/(εs ε0), ε0 = 8.854×10⁻¹⁴ F/cm, εs = 11.7 (Si). n-side, ρ = qN_D: dE/dx = qN_D/(εsε0) (the notes' sign convention) ⇒ **E(x) = (qN_D/εsε0)(W_n − x)**, linear, zero at W_n. **|E_max| = qN_D W_n/(εsε0) = qN_A W_p/(εsε0)** at the junction (x = 0). **V_n = qN_D W_n²/(2εsε0), V_p = qN_A W_p²/(2εsε0), V_bi = V_n + V_p.** Substitute W_p = W N_D/(N_A + N_D), W_n = W N_A/(N_A + N_D) ⇒ **W = √((2εsε0 V_bi/q)(1/N_A + 1/N_D))**. W ∝ √V_bi, ∝ 1/√N. Very high doping (10¹⁸) gives W ≈ 10–20 nm and tunnelling (**tunnel diode**).

### 11_PN Junction diode under bias (typed image, 8 pp)
Non-equilibrium. **Quasi-Fermi levels E_Fn, E_Fp** (local Fermi energy for electrons and holes); **their separation across the junction = qV_a**; they merge into E_F at V_a = 0. **Forward** (+ to p): majority carriers pushed across (injection); uncovered charge falls, so W → W − ΔW; barrier **q(V_bi − V_a)** (e.g. 0.76 − 0.4 = 0.36 V); large current. Injected carriers become minority, recombine and decay, giving diffusion current; J_p and J_n both flow +x (p → n). p_n0 = n_i²/N_D; **p_n(W_n) = p_n0 e^{qV_a/kT}**; Δp(W_n) = p_n0(e^{qV_a/kT} − 1); **long diode** (neutral length ≫ L_p): Δp(x) = Δp(W_n) e^{−(x−W_n)/L_p}; **short diode: linear decay instead.** J_p(W_n) = qD_p(−dΔp/dx) = q(D_p/L_p)p_n0(e^{qV_a/kT} − 1); J_n(−W_p) = q(D_n/L_n)n_p0(…). **Kirchhoff: total current is constant in x;** the decaying diffusion current is made up by **majority-carrier drift** in the quasi-neutral regions; **no recombination in the depletion region assumed**, so J_total = J_p(W_n) + J_n(−W_p). **J0 = qn_i²(D_p/(L_p N_D) + D_n/(L_n N_A))**, independent of V and x. Example: J0 = 1 fA at 0.6 V gives about 100 μA. Turn-on voltage relates to V_bi and E_G. **Reverse** (− to p): carriers pulled away; W → W + ΔW; barrier q(V_bi + V_R) (0.76 + 2 = 2.76 V); e^{qV_a/kT} → 0 even at −0.3 V, so J ≈ −J0. Origin: thermally generated minority carriers reaching the depletion edge are swept across; **minority concentration ≈ 0 at the depletion edge** in reverse; pA–fA. **J0 ∝ n_i²:** Ge (0.7 eV) leaks a lot, GaN (3.4 eV) very little, so wide gap gives better **rectification** (power devices, photodetector dark current). Rectifier: large forward, −J0 reverse.

### 12_Generation and recombination currents in PN junction diode (typed image, 7 pp)
J0 depends strongly on the band gap, which makes the diode a rectifier. **G-R currents** come from defects, traps and dislocations; the effect matters most **in the depletion region** (low p and n). **J_total = J_ideal + J_GR.** SRH: U = (Pn − n_i²)/((P + n + 2n_i)τ); **U > 0 means net recombination (forward); U < 0 means net generation (reverse).**
**Recombination current (forward), assumptions:** mid-gap traps (E_t = E_i, maximum recombination); P, n ≫ n_i; maximum when P ≈ n; **Pn = n_i² e^{qVa/kT}** ⇒ P ≈ n ≈ n_i e^{qVa/2kT} ⇒ denominator ≈ 2n_i e^{qVa/2kT} ⇒ **U_rec ≈ (n_i/2τ) e^{qVa/2kT}** ⇒ **J_rec = q∫U dx ≈ (qn_i W/2τ) e^{qVa/2kT}**.
**Ideality factor η:** ideal diffusion η = 1, recombination η = 2 (J ∝ e^{qV/ηkT}). Low V: J_rec dominates, slope of log J vs V ∝ 1/2kT; high V: J_ideal (slope 1/kT) dominates. Poor material (badly grown GaN) can give η of 5–10.
**Generation current (reverse):** P, n ≪ n_i ⇒ **U_gen ≈ −n_i/2τ** (net generation; not optical, from traps) ⇒ **J_gen = qW n_i/(2τ)**; J_rec = J_gen e^{qVa/2kT}. **J_rev = −J0 − J_gen**: J0 is constant but J_gen ∝ W ∝ √(V_b + V_R), so **real reverse current rises slowly until breakdown.**
**Band gap vs turn-on voltage:** V_on ≈ V_bi; Ge 0.7 eV → ~0.4 V; Si 1.1 → 0.7–0.8 V; GaAs 1.42 → 1.0–1.1 V; GaN 3.4 → ~3.2 V. Same current (20 mA) in GaN needs more voltage, hence more power. **LED requirements:** forward bias, direct gap (GaAs, GaN), band-to-band radiative recombination (trap recombination gives heat). **λ = hc/E_G:** GaN 3.4 eV → ≈ 365 nm (near UV/violet); wider gap, shorter wavelength (blue/UV); narrow gap, IR/red. Shorter λ needs a higher turn-on voltage (more energy for the same brightness).

### 13_BJT1 (typed image, 7 pp)
Three-terminal device: the third point C controls the A–B current independently of V_AB. Applications: **switching** (power electronics: block 1000 V or pass 10 A with ~0.01 V control; DC-DC converters, inverters, motor drives, EV chargers), logic (NAND/NOR, ICs), memory, **amplifiers** (power amplifiers, LNAs at GHz). Bipolar = electrons and holes; two back-to-back p–n junctions; E injects, B controls, C collects; NPN and PNP. Active mode: EB forward-biased (W↓, barrier↓, inject e⁻ E→B and holes B→E), BC reverse-biased (W↑, huge field). NPN working: injection → transport and recombination (minority electrons in the base) → collection (field sweeps electrons into C) → control: **I_C is set by injection (V_BE) and base transport, not by V_C.** Currents: I_E = I_En + I_Ep (I_En useful, I_Ep useless); I_B = I_B,rec + I_Ep (back-injected); I_C = I_nc + I_BC,reverse (pA–fA, neglected) ≈ I_nc. **B = I_nc/I_En**, **γ_E = I_En/(I_En + I_Ep) = I_En/I_E** (e.g. 0.99), **α = I_C/I_E ≈ I_nc/I_E = B·γ_E** (common-base gain), **β = I_C/I_B = α/(1 − α)** (common-emitter gain; higher β such as 99 is better; keep I_B small). Design: **metallurgical base width W_B** (junction depth) vs **neutral base width W_Bn** (after EB and BC depletion; the crucial one); narrow base gives high B. **Emitter N+, base P−:** current across a junction is inversely proportional to the doping on the side minority carriers are injected into.

### 14_BJT2 (typed image, 8 pp)
Forward active: B–E forward (V_BE), B–C reverse (V_BC). **Control mechanism:** a small change in I_B gives a small ΔV_BE, and since I_E depends exponentially on V_BE, I_C changes greatly: amplification. Current components as above. **Requirements:** N_DE ≫ N_AB and narrow W_bn. **Minority profile in the base:** short base, so **≈ linear**. Boundary conditions: **n_b(0) = (n_i²/N_AB)e^{qV_BE/kT}** (EB edge); **n_b(W_bn) ≈ (n_i²/N_AB)e^{−qV_BC/kT} ≈ 0** (BC edge, reverse). Exact currents (hyperbolic):
- **I_E = [qA(D_n/L_n)(n_i²/N_AB)coth(W_bn/L_n) + qA(D_e/L_e)(n_i²/N_DE)](e^{qV_BE/kT} − 1)** (first term I_En, second I_Ep; D_e, L_e = holes in the emitter).
- **I_C = qA(D_n/L_n)(n_i²/N_AB)(1/sinh(W_bn/L_n))(e^{qV_BE/kT} − 1).**
- **I_B = I_E − I_C = I_Ep + I_rec**; **I_Ep = qA(D_e/L_e)(n_i²/N_DE)(e^{qV_BE/kT} − 1)**; **I_rec ≈ qA(D_n/(2L_n²))(n_i²/N_AB)W_Bn(e^{qV_BE/kT} − 1).**

Metrics: **γ ≈ 1 − (N_AB D_e W_bn)/(N_DE D_n L_e)**; **B = 1/cosh(W_bn/L_n) ≈ 1 − W_bn²/(2L_n²)**; α = Bγ; **β ≈ (D_n L_e N_DE)/(D_e W_bn N_AB)** (back-injection dominated); **β ≈ 2L_n²/W_bn²** (recombination dominated). Maximise β: raise N_DE, lower N_AB, shrink W_bn. **CB configuration:** base grounded, input I_E, output I_C, α = I_C/I_E (5 mA in → 4.9 mA out); I_C vs V_CB curves flat, stepping with I_E. **CE:** emitter grounded, I_B (μA) in, I_C (mA) out; 5 μA gives 5 mA; β = I_C/I_B; I_C vs V_CE. **Early effect:** lightly doped base, so the BC depletion expands into the base as V_CB/V_CE rises; W_bn falls and I_C rises; curves slope (resistor-like). **Early voltage −V_A** where the curves meet; ideally infinite. **Trade-off:** a large V_A needs a heavily doped base, but that lowers γ and β (**β vs V_A trade-off**). **Gummel number Q_G ≈ N_AB·W_bn** (majority charge in the neutral base); **V_A ∝ Q_G/C_BC**, C_BC = ε0εs A/W_BC; raise V_A with higher N_AB or a lower C_BC, i.e. a wide BC depletion, so **the collector must be very lightly doped.**

## 2_MOS Capacitor, MOSFET, CMOS

### MOS Capacitor_1
MOSFET is the most common transistor (processors, memory). The MOS capacitor is its building block: metal (Al, Ni; gate V_G) / oxide (SiO₂, thickness d, perfect insulator) / semiconductor (p or n, ohmic contact, grounded). **Ideal flat band:** φ_m = φ_s, so the Fermi levels align and the bands are flat at V_G = 0. p-type: E_F near E_v; E_i mid-gap; **bulk potential φ_F (ψ_BP) = E_i − E_F** (strength of p-doping). Metal–semiconductor Fermi-level difference = qV_G. **Accumulation** (V_G < 0): holes pile up; **bands bend up**; E_F gets closer to E_v at the surface. **Depletion** (small V_G > 0): holes repelled, depletion region, surface less p-type; **bands bend down**; E_c moves toward E_F. **Inversion** (large V_G > 0): electron channel; E_i at the surface crosses E_F. **Weak inversion** begins when E_i(surface) reaches E_F (band bending = φ_F). **Strong inversion:** E_i(surface) is above E_F by φ_F, so surface electron density = bulk hole density; **total band bending = 2φ_F**.

### MOS Capacitor_2 (C–V)
Table: V_G < 0 accumulation, small > 0 depletion, ≫ 0 inversion. Accumulation layer (thin, high hole density). Depletion leaves fixed acceptor ions. Strong inversion: surface n = bulk p; the inversion charge sheet plus the depletion charge is balanced by a thin positive gate sheet. C measured with a small AC signal on the DC bias (ΔQ/ΔV). **C_ox = ε_ox/t_ox**, the constant maximum. **Accumulation:** majority holes respond fast, modulation at the interface, C = C_ox. **Depletion:** C_D = ε_s/W_D in series with C_ox; V_G ↑, W_D ↑, C_D ↓, C ↓. **Strong inversion:** W_D reaches **W_D,max** (the inversion layer screens the field), giving C_min. **Low frequency (~100 Hz):** minority electrons have time to be thermally generated and follow, so C rises back to C_ox. **High frequency (kHz–MHz):** they can't follow; holes at the edge of W_max respond; **C stays at C_min**. **Deep depletion:** if the **DC** sweep is very fast, no inversion layer forms, W keeps growing and C drops below C_min. **Flat-band voltage V_FB:** ideal (φ_m = φ_s) flat at V_G = 0; real: the V_G needed to flatten the bands. **Debye length L_D:** the AC signal modulates charge to a small depth even at flat band; **C_Debye = ε_s/L_D**; **C_FB = C_ox in series with C_Debye**.
Standard: L_D = √(ε_s kT/(q² N_A)).

### MOSFET_1 (basics)
CMOS digital: 0 = GND, 1 = V_DD; analog = time-dependent signals. Goal: easy switching, high speed, low power. FET: the gate field controls S→D current; ON = conducting, OFF = not. Terminals S, D, G plus substrate (bulk): 4 terminals. Gate = metal or **polysilicon**; SiO₂; p-substrate (NMOS); n⁺ S/D (heavily doped); metal contacts. **t_ox:** thinner means a stronger field and a lower threshold. **L_drawn, lateral diffusion L_D, L_eff = L_drawn − 2L_D.** V_DS, V_GS; source grounded (V_GS = V_G); bulk bias V_SB; NMOS bulk → most negative (often GND); PMOS bulk → most positive. NMOS: electrons, positive V_G and V_D, p-sub, n⁺ S/D. PMOS: holes, negative V_G and V_D, n-sub, p⁺ S/D. Enhancement (normally off; the course focus) vs depletion (normally on). **V_T** = gate voltage at which the channel inverts; cut-off at V_G = 0 (holes, no path); positive V_G repels holes and attracts electrons; depletion, then inversion at V_T (electrons = original hole concentration). V_T is set by material and doping, fixed for a finished device. I_D vs V_DS; **R_on** when V_GS > V_T; changing biases changes R_on; **delay τ = R × C**.

### MOSFET_2 (I–V)
Drift current. I = Q × v; v = μE; E = −dV/dx. Initially S, D grounded: only a transverse field. V_T = onset of inversion, **≈ 0.5 V for an enhancement NMOS**. **Overdrive = V_GS − V_T** controls the inversion charge, Q ∝ C_ox W(V_GS − V_T). With V_DS: the channel potential V(x) rises 0 → V_DS ("potential divider"), so Q(x) ∝ (V_GS − V_T − V(x)) and the sheet thins. **Pinch-off:** the drain/substrate n⁺/p junction is reverse-biased, and the large depletion at the drain thins the channel. **Gradual channel approximation (GCA):** E along the channel ≪ E across it; holds for long channels and fails when L is ~0.1 μm. **Charge-sheet approximation:** inversion layer of zero thickness. **Linear/triode (V_DS < V_GS − V_T):** I_D = μ_n C_ox (W/L)[(V_GS − V_T)V_DS − V_DS²/2]; W/L = aspect ratio; parabolic; small V_DS gives Ohm's law (a voltage-variable resistor); **R_on = 1/[μ_n C_ox(W/L)(V_GS − V_T)]**, inversely proportional to overdrive. **Saturation (V_DS ≥ V_GS − V_T):** the peak of the parabola is at V_DS = V_GS − V_T (pinch-off/saturation point); **I_D,max = (μ_n C_ox/2)(W/L)(V_GS − V_T)²**, independent of V_DS: an **ideal current source**. **Sub-threshold:** a smooth transition with current below V_T; to switch off, V_G must go well below V_T; **sub-threshold slope S** = gate swing for one decade of I_D (e.g. 10⁻² → 10⁻³ A), in mV/decade, **ideal 60 mV/decade**.

### MOSFET_3 (short-channel effects, scaling, models)
Moore's law (here: doubling every ~1.5 years); to double density, halve the dimensions; **scaling** = reduce L, W, t_ox; **SCEs** become prominent. **Constant-field scaling:** L 1 → 0.5 μm needs V_DS 1 → 0.5 V; reduce t_ox together with V_GS. **Constant-voltage scaling:** voltages kept constant, "often preferred". Second-order effects: **body effect:** NMOS body at the most negative bias; V_B < 0 keeps S-B and D-B junctions reverse-biased (V_B > 0 would forward-bias them: heavy current, burnout); more negative V_B makes V_SB more positive, the depletion thicker, so a larger V_G is needed: **V_T ↑**. **CLM:** in saturation the drain-bulk depletion eats into the channel; L_eff = L − ΔL; I_D rises slightly with V_DS; not an ideal current source; **finite r_o**; factor (1 + λV_DS). **Velocity saturation:** v ∝ E, then non-linear, then constant v_sat beyond the **critical field E_c**; scattering balances the drive; in short channels **V_DSAT < V_GS − V_T** and **I_D becomes linear in (V_GS − V_T)**, independent of V_DS. **DIBL:** a positive drain lowers E_c at the drain side; in a short channel the drain lowers the source-end barrier ("hump"), so **V_T ↓**. **Punch-through:** n⁺ S and D close over the p-substrate, depletion regions touch, a direct path, can't be turned off. **Manual models:** off for V_GS ≤ V_T; linear (resistor); **process transconductance k′ = μ_n C_ox**, **k = k′(W/L)**; I_D with (1 + λV_DS). **Five parameters for manual analysis: k′, λ, V_T, γ (body coefficient), V_DSAT.**

### CMOS_1 (inverter basics I)
The "main workout" of digital VLSI. 1 = V_DD, 0 = GND. **M2 = PMOS pull-up (PU), M1 = NMOS pull-down (PD).** PMOS source to V_DD, NMOS source to GND, gates tied to V_in, drains tied to V_out. Table: V_in = 0 → M1 off, M2 on, V_out = V_DD (M2 charges C_load); V_in = V_DD → M1 on, M2 off, V_out = 0 (M1 discharges C_load). **Switch model:** V_in high → R_N discharges C_L; V_in low → R_P charges C_L. **C_L** = internal device capacitances (gate-drain of both) + input capacitance of the next stage. First-order ODE gives the RC time constant (delay). **Static behaviour:** (A) very small static power (no V_DD-GND path); (B) **high input impedance** (oxide); **fan-out:** static fan-out ideally infinite; **dynamic fan-out** degrades speed (larger C_L); **low output impedance** (always tied to V_DD or GND): less noise, high SNR; (C) **ratioless logic / full swing:** the static 0/1 levels are independent of the W/L of PMOS and NMOS; output reaches the full V_DD and 0. **VTC:** V_out vs V_in from 0 to V_DD; **switching threshold V_M** = input where the transition happens; found where **I_Dn = I_Dp** (load-line intersection); **V_GSn = V_in, V_DSn = V_out, V_GSp = V_in − V_DD, V_DSp = V_out − V_DD.** **Noise margin:** intrinsic noise rejection (noise sources: 1/f, shot, wiring, junction); designed via W/L, V_DD, V_T. **Gain = ΔV_out/ΔV_in:** high gain enables analog amplification and faster digital switching.

## 3_Manufacturing (notes)

### Crystal growth process (CZ)
The Czochralski technique is one of two methods (the other is **float zone**) to draw single-crystal Si from **polysilicon = electronic-grade silicon (EGS), ~99.999999999 % pure**, crushed into pieces called **the charge**. Also called the "liquid–solid mono-component growth system"; **invented 1918**. Parts: **quartz/silica crucible** (inner; cracks on cooling so not reusable; adds O₂, which is a contaminant but improves mechanical strength); **graphite susceptor** (outer; carbon contamination); **graphite heater** (RF induction or resistance heating); quartz **chamber** (evacuated, then **argon**); **crucible shaft rotates clockwise**; **pull/seed shaft rotates anticlockwise**, holding a **seed crystal** with orientation ⟨111⟩ or ⟨100⟩. Steps: load, evacuate, Ar; melt at **≈1412 °C**; lower the seed to touch the melt; pull up slowly; Si solidifies at the interface. **Controls:** counter-rotation (uniform melt temperature, no hot/cold spots); **pull rate** of a few mm/min **sets the diameter** (too slow and the seed melts); the crystal copies the seed orientation. Product: **ingot**, 8–16 inch diameters (large-diameter advantage); sliced into **0.50–0.75 mm** thick wafers. Disadvantages: **O from the crucible, C from the susceptor**.

### Oxidation
Si + O₂ (or H₂O) at high T gives SiO₂. The Si/SiO₂ interface is one of the best electrical interfaces, a key reason silicon dominates. SiO₂: excellent insulator, **band gap ~9 eV**, high breakdown field. Uses: **gate dielectric** (thin), **field/isolation oxide** (thick; raises V_T to stop parasitic paths), **dopant masking**. Equipment: oxidation/diffusion furnaces (quartz tube, heating coils; horizontal or vertical: vertical has a smaller footprint and symmetric gas flow). Procedure: a boat of 50–100 wafers loaded slowly (thermal shock), inert flush, ramp to **~900–1000 °C**, oxidise, ramp down, pull out slowly. Rate is exponential in T (Arrhenius), so uniform T is critical. Reaction at the **oxide/Si interface**; the oxidant must **diffuse through the existing oxide**; Si is consumed; **volume grows ×2.2** (standard: 44 % of the oxide thickness is consumed Si). **Dry (O₂):** dense, electrically superior (gate oxide). **Wet (H₂O):** hydroxyl groups, porous, inferior but fast (thick oxides). **Deal–Grove model:** flux F₁ diffusion through the oxide, F₂ reaction at the interface. **Linear regime** (thin oxide, reaction-limited): x ∝ t. **Parabolic regime** (thick, diffusion-limited): x² ∝ t. Standard: x² + Ax = B(t + τ); linear rate B/A, parabolic rate B. **Tunnelling** through ultra-thin t_ox gives leakage; the solution is **high-k** (Hf-based oxides) **deposited by ALD** instead of grown, raising C_ox = κε0/t without a thinner oxide (SiO₂ κ ≈ 3.9).

### Doping: diffusion and ion implantation
Doping control is needed vertically and laterally; S/D doping sets the **parasitic resistance** (lower R gives more drive current); channel doping controls SCEs (punch-through); hard in FinFETs and nanosheets. **Thermal diffusion** (1960s–80s): horizontal quartz furnace tubes, wafers loaded slowly, **800–1000 °C**; sources **phosphorus oxychloride (POCl₃)** for n and **boron** for p. **Dopants must be substitutional to be active** (interstitial ones are inactive). **Masking: SiO₂** (dopant diffusivity much lower in oxide). **Fick's 1st law:** J = −D dC/dx; **Fick's 2nd law:** ∂C/∂t = D ∂²C/∂x². **Arrhenius D = D₀ exp(−E_a/kT).** **Solid solubility limit C_s.** Two steps: **predeposition** (constant surface concentration C_s, unlimited source, **erfc** profile, dose **Q ∝ C_s√(Dt)**); **drive-in** (limited source, fixed dose, surface concentration falls with time, **Gaussian** profile); **diffusion length √(Dt)**.
**Ion implantation** (since ~1980s): precise, **low temperature**, many mask choices (metal, thick SiO₂, photoresist); **peak below the surface**. System: **ionizer** (electrons or plasma) → **mass separator (analysing magnet)** → **accelerator** (energy sets depth) → **scanning/focusing** (X/Y electrostatic) → **neutral beam trap** (neutrals can't be steered) → **Faraday cup** (dose measurement). Ions lose energy (nuclear and electronic stopping). **Projected range R_p** = mean depth; **Gaussian profile**; **straggle ΔR_p** = standard deviation. **Light ions (B): deeper, broader. Heavy (Sb): shallower, narrower** (raise the energy to go deeper). **Mask thickness** must keep the penetrated concentration ≤ the substrate background. Comparison table: diffusion = high T, peak at surface, erfc/Gaussian, deep junctions set by D and t, SiO₂ mask, **minimal damage** (equilibrium); implantation = low T but **needs an anneal** (activate: interstitial → substitutional, repair **crystal damage**), precise, peak at R_p, depth set by **energy (keV–MeV)**, many masks, complex system.

### Lithography
Pattern printing: **film deposition → resist → exposure (mask, scanner) → development → etch/implant → resist removal**, repeated many times. Moore's law (1965; doubling every ~2 years at similar cost); first commercial monolithic IC (1961) had **4 transistors**; **Nvidia Hopper (2022) 80 billion**. **Positive resist:** exposed areas become soluble and are removed, so the pattern equals the mask; higher resolution, better dimension control and alignment. **Negative resist:** exposed areas cross-link and become insoluble, so the unexposed areas are removed and the pattern is the inverse; higher etch resistance, thicker films, lower resolution. **Rayleigh resolution R = k₁λ/NA** (NA = sine of the lens's half-angle; k₁ = process factor). To improve: ↓λ, ↑NA, ↓k₁. λ: mercury arc lamps (broad spectrum, chromatic aberration) → **excimer lasers** → **DUV 193 nm (ArF)** → **EUV 13.5 nm** (tin droplets hit by an IR laser make a plasma). NA: aspherical lenses (beyond ~0.8); **immersion lithography** (water raises NA by the refractive index, practical limit **~1.35**; the DUV workhorse). k₁: **computational lithography, OPC** (pre-distort the mask; free-form OPC; sub-resolution assist features), **annular/dipole illumination**, **FlexRay** programmable micromirrors, **phase-shift masks**, **multiple patterning**.

### Basic fabrication steps for NMOS (metal gate)
Wafers 125/150/200/300 mm; shaped, sawn, lapped, etched, **CMP**. Cleaning after nearly every step. **Five categories:** wafer preparation (**CZ**; **Bridgman for GaAs**), oxide formation (dry/wet), patterning (lithography, etch, resist, mask), doping (diffusion/implant), deposition (**CVD, sputtering, vacuum evaporation**). Not strictly sequential.
**NMOS metal-gate flow:**
1. p-substrate.
2. Grow SiO₂ mask (900–1200 °C).
3. Positive photoresist.
4. Mask 1 + UV exposes the S/D windows; develop.
5. **Etch SiO₂ with HF.**
6. **Strip resist with piranha (H₂SO₄:H₂O₂ 3:1).**
7. Diffuse/implant the **n⁺ S/D**.
8. Regrow oxide.
9. Mask 2 opens the gate region.
10. Grow **thin gate oxide**.
11. Mask 3 opens the S/D contacts (keeping the gate oxide).
12. **Metal (Al) over everything.**
13. Mask 4 patterns the metal to isolate S, D, G.
14. **Overglass/passivation**, leaving the bond pads open.

CZ figures: crucible clockwise, seed counter-clockwise, Ar, vacuum pump, silica crucible, graphite susceptor and heater, thermal shield; the melt is held near **1417 °C** (other notes 1412 °C; accepted Si melting point 1414 °C); **pulled crystals contain O ≈ 10¹⁸ and C ≈ 10¹⁶ cm⁻³**; essentially all IC wafers are CZ; control parameters are **pull rate, melt temperature, rotation rate**.

Wet: Si + 2H₂O → SiO₂ + 2H₂.** **LOCOS** (local oxidation of silicon) isolation image. **Deal–Grove fluxes:** **F₁ = h_G(C_G − C_S)** (gas to surface), **F₂ = −D dC/dx = D(C* − C_i)/x₀** (diffusion through the oxide), **F₃ = k_S C_i** (interface reaction). Thin oxide: x₀ = (B/A)(t + τ), linear, reaction-limited; thick: **x₀² = B(t + τ)**, parabolic, diffusion-limited (C_i ≈ 0). Doping effects: higher channel doping reduces DIBL and suppresses punch-through (shorter depletion). Fick's laws work for low concentrations only (fail at high doping or during oxidation). ### Notes_MOSFET_SPICE_Models
**SPICE = Simulation Program with Integrated Circuit Emphasis** (UC Berkeley, ~1972/73). Fabrication is expensive and irreversible, so simulate first. EDA vendors: Cadence, Synopsys, Mentor Graphics, Tanner. **Model file** = text of technology parameters; **PTM** (Predictive Technology Models) cover 0.18 μm down to 90/65/45/32/22/16 nm; all devices in one circuit must use the same technology. **SPICE equivalent circuit:** 4 terminals; R_D, R_S; S-B and D-B junction diodes; C_gs, C_gd, C_gb; C_db, C_sb. **Model table:** Level 1 **Shichman–Hodges** square law, ~7 parameters, for hand calculations, inaccurate below **L < 0.8 μm**; Level 2 physics-based (SCE, subthreshold, field-dependent mobility; complex, slow, convergence problems); Level 3 semi-empirical (faster convergence, loses accuracy at sub-micron); **Level 4/54 BSIM** (deep submicron, >100 parameters, not for hand analysis). Hand calculation uses Level 1; EDA uses BSIM.

## 5_Semiconductor memories

### Board notes (1112-1600.pdf, 13 pp; some pages show unrelated power-systems board work)
**Memory** = an electronic circuit that stores data; **bit** = the smallest unit. Performance: **area efficiency, access time (speed), power (static, dynamic)**: area, speed, power. Classification by (i) type of data storage and (ii) type of access: **ROM** (read only; data stored at fabrication; non-volatile; mask-programmed ROM, PROM, EPROM, EEPROM) vs **read/write memory (RAM)** (rewritable; volatile; no sequential access needed; SRAM, DRAM). **Organisation:** each memory cell stores 1 bit, arranged in an array (memory core) plus peripherals. **Typical RAM:** row decoder (A1…AN) drives the word lines (rows 1…2^N); bit lines are the columns (1…2^M) into the column decoder; **capacity 2^N × 2^M bits** (the board writes 2^m × 2^n); word lines are horizontal lines connecting all cells in a row; bit lines are vertical lines used to read/write a specific cell; **both the word line and the bit line must be activated to read/write**.
**Static CMOS design:** inverter (A = 0 → out 1; A = 1 → out 0); general structure: a PUN of pMOS from V_DD and a PDN of nMOS to GND, with C_load. Delays t_PLH (0→1) and t_PHL (1→0); design for **t_PLH = t_PHL**, i.e. μn C_ox(W/L)_n = μp C_ox(W/L)_p, so **(W/L)_p = (μn/μp)(W/L)_n ≈ 2–3×**. **Method:** (1) design the PDN: **OR = parallel nMOS, AND = series nMOS**; (2) PUN = dual: **parallel in PDN becomes series pMOS in PUN, series becomes parallel**; (3) the output is the **inverted** form. **Example 1: 2-input NOR** Y = (A+B)′: M1, M2 pMOS in series (PUN), M3, M4 nMOS in parallel (PDN); truth table 00→1 (M1, M2 on; M3, M4 off), 01→0, 10→0, 11→0. **Example 2: 2-input NAND** Y = (AB)′: pMOS M1, M2 in parallel, nMOS M3, M4 in series; 00→1, 01→1, 10→1, 11→0. **Pseudo-nMOS:** a single pMOS with its gate grounded (always on) as the load, plus an nMOS PDN (e.g. A, B parallel = NOR). **Example 3: Y = (AB + CD)′:** PDN = (A series B) parallel (C series D); PUN = (A parallel B) series (C parallel D). **ROM circuits: 4-bit × 4-bit NOR-based ROM:** pMOS pull-ups with grounded gates on columns C1–C4 (bit lines) to V_DD; rows R1–R4 (word lines) from the row decoder drive the nMOS gates; an nMOS at (Ri, Cj) pulls Cj to 0 when Ri = 1; no transistor leaves Cj = 1. Example rows: R1 = 1 (others 0) gives C1–C4 = 0 1 0 1 [board: "0 1 0 1"]; R3 = 1 gives 1 0 0 1 [board]. Column decoder below.

### Semiconductor_Memories_Lecture_Notes (typed)
Definition; 1-bit cell replicated in an array; MOS-based (density, power, speed). **DFT:** test vectors; exhaustive testing is impossible with thousands of inputs (input-vector explosion), so use random samples; **fault modelling**. **Metrics table:** storage density (bits/area; tapes and floppies vs SD cards up to 512 GB); **cost per bit** (fewer transistors per cell); access time; static power (standby); dynamic power (switching 0↔1); **data retention time**. **Classification:** ROM (non-volatile, keeps data without power, mostly reads: **Mask ROM, PROM (one-time field programmable), EPROM (erase and rewrite many times), EEPROM (electrically erasable), Flash (high-density electrically erasable)**; write cycles limited and slower than reads) vs RAM (volatile; fast read and write; **SRAM: cross-coupled transistors hold state while powered; DRAM: charge storage plus periodic refresh**). Array architecture: row decoder asserts **1 of 2^N word lines**; column decoder picks from **2^M bit lines**; each intersection is a bit. **4×4 NOR ROM:** R1–R4 to the nMOS gates; C1–C4 with pull-ups (PMOS or depletion nMOS loads); default state all 1; selected row turns on its nMOS, discharging the connected columns to 0; no transistor leaves 1; **programmed by placing or omitting transistors at fabrication**.
