I. The Influence of Moore’s Law and Device Scaling
The ongoing reduction in the size of transistors is driven by Moore’s Law, which predicted that the number
of transistors per unit area on a chip would double approximately every one and a half years.
1. Requirement for Doubling: To double the transistor density, the only option is to reduce the physical
dimensions of the device by half,.
2. Scaling Definition: Scaling involves the reduction of overall device dimensions, specifically the
channel length (
), the width (
), and the gate oxide thickness (
),.
3. Short Channel Effects (SCEs): As dimensions are reduced, certain physical phenomena become much
more prominent in the electrical characteristics (IV characteristics). These phenomena are known
collectively as Short Channel Effects (SCEs),. SCEs affect the overall functionality of the system and
must be understood to ensure Moore’s Law remains valid.
Scaling Theories (How Dimensions are Reduced)
Scaling not only involves reducing physical size but also voltages and currents. Two main types of scaling
theories exist:
1. Constant Electric Field Scaling Theory: This approach ensures the electric field within the device
remains constant.

4. Drain Induced Barrier Lowering (DIBL)
DIBL (often referred to as ‘dibble’) is the effect where the drain voltage lowers the energy barrier near the
source.
Conduction Band Diagram: Applying a positive voltage to the drain lowers the conduction band
energy on the drain side.
Mechanism: Because the channel is short, the drain is physically close to the source. The drain voltage
influences the energy barrier (or “hump”) that electrons must overcome to enter the channel from the
source,.
Result: The drain effectively lowers the barrier at the source end. Since the barrier is lowered, a
lower gate voltage is required to switch on the device. This means DIBL leads to a reduction in the
threshold voltage (
).
5. Punch Through
Punch through is a severe issue resulting from extremely short channels.
Mechanism: When source (
) and drain (
) regions are brought too close together over a -type
substrate, their respective depletion regions may meet and touch.
Result: This connection creates a direct, unintended electrical path between the source and the drain.
Current can flow through this path even if the gate voltage is lowered significantly (turned off),
because a permanent path is available,. This makes it impossible to fully turn off the device.
