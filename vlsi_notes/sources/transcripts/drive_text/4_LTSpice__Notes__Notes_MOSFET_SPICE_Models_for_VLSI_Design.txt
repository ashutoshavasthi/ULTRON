MOSFET SPICE Models for VLSI Design 
1. Introduction to Circuit Simulation and EDA Tools 
1.1 The Role of SPICE in VLSI Design 
• 
SPICE Definition: SPICE stands for Simulation Program with Integrated Circuit Emphasis, originally 
developed at the University of California, Berkeley in the 1970s (around 1972). 
• 
Purpose: In VLSI design, physical fabrication and post-fabrication testing of integrated circuits are 
expensive and irreversible; if an integrated circuit fails to meet specifications after fabrication, all silicon 
and manufacturing resources are lost. 
• 
EDA Tool Integration: Device behavior is described mathematically in software known as Electronic 
Design Automation (EDA) tools. Common industry-standard EDA vendors include Cadence, Synopsys, 
Mentor Graphics, and Tanner. 
• 
Function of Device Models: EDA tools require compact mathematical representations of device 
behavior—called MOSFET models—to calculate terminal voltages, current equations (especially drain 
current 𝐼𝐷), charges, and circuit responses under applied inputs. 
1.2 Model Files and Predictive Technology Models (PTM) 
• 
Model Files: A model file is a text-based configuration file that defines physical constants and 
technology-dependent parameters for a target process. These files serve as inputs to the simulator so the 
mathematical equations inside the tool can compute current and voltage characteristics. 
• 
Predictive Technology Models (PTM): Model libraries (such as PTM) provide standardized model 
parameter files across various technology generations, ranging from older submicron processes (e.g., 
0.18 𝜇m) down to deep submicron nodes (e.g., 90 nm, 65 nm, 45 nm, 32 nm, 22 nm, 16 nm). 
• 
Consistency Requirement: For a given circuit simulation, all interconnected MOSFET devices must use 
consistent technology model definitions corresponding to that fabrication process. 
2. SPICE MOSFET Equivalent Circuit and Parasitic Components 
A complete SPICE model goes beyond ideal channel equations by incorporating internal parasitic elements 
and substrate interactions: 
• 
Terminal Terminals: Four terminals—Gate (G), Drain (D), Source (S), and Bulk/Body (B). 
• 
Extrinsic Resistances: Parasitic series resistances at the drain (𝑅𝐷) and source (𝑅𝑆). 
• 
Junction Diodes: Reverse-biased p-n junction diodes between the source-to-bulk and drain-to-bulk 
junctions. 
• 
Parasitic Capacitances:Terminal-to-terminal capacitances: Gate-to-Source (𝐶𝑔𝑠), Gate-to-
Drain (𝐶𝑔𝑑), Gate-to-Bulk (𝐶𝑔𝑏). 
• 
Junction capacitances: Drain-to-Bulk (𝐶𝑑𝑏) and Source-to-Bulk (𝐶𝑠𝑏) depletion capacitances.

4.2 Key Second-Order Physical Effects in Levels 2 and 3 
When channel lengths drop below 0.8 𝜇m, hand-calculation assumptions break down and advanced models 
incorporate the following second-order effects: 
A. Carrier Velocity Saturation and Scattering 
• 
In long-channel devices, drift velocity is proportional to the electric field (𝑣= 𝜇ℰ). 
• 
In short-channel devices, high lateral electric fields cause carrier velocity to saturate due to optical 
phonon scattering (𝑣𝑠𝑎𝑡). Consequently, the drain current saturates at lower values and earlier than 
predicted by classical channel pinch-off. 
B. Channel Length Modulation (𝛥𝐿) 
• 
In saturation, the depletion region at the drain junction widens, reducing the effective channel length by 
𝛥𝐿. 
• 
Advanced models treat the variation in channel length not merely as a simple empirical constant 𝜆, but 
as a dynamic function dependent on 𝑉𝐷𝑆, doping profiles, and junction depths. 
C. Subthreshold Conduction (Weak Inversion) 
• 
Weak Inversion Regime: When 𝑉𝐺𝑆< 𝑉𝑇, the transistor does not turn off abruptly. 
• 
Conduction Mechanism: Current in the subthreshold regime is governed primarily by carrier diffusion 
between the source and the drain rather than drift. 
• 
Impact of Scaling: In deep submicron transistors, subthreshold leakage current increases significantly and 
becomes a critical contributor to static power consumption. 
•

5. Modern State-of-the-Art Compact Models 
5.1 BSIM (Berkeley Short-channel IGFET Model) 
• 
Design Philosophy: Developed to provide an accurate, computationally robust model for submicron and 
deep submicron VLSI design. 
• 
Parameter Extraction: Combines physical equations with empirical fitting parameters extracted directly 
from experimental silicon measurements. 
• 
Industrial Adoption: BSIM models (e.g., Level 4 / Level 54 and subsequent BSIM releases) are widely 
adopted across commercial silicon foundries and standard EDA design flows.

5.2 EKV Model (Enz-Krummenacher-Vittoz) 
• 
Addressing Regional Discontinuities: Conventional piecewise SPICE models define separate equations for 
weak inversion (𝑉𝐺𝑆< 𝑉𝑇), moderate inversion, and strong inversion (𝑉𝐺𝑆> 𝑉𝑇). This often causes non-
physical derivative discontinuities at transition boundaries, degrading simulation convergence. 
• 
Unified Equation: The EKV model introduces a single, continuous formulation that spans seamlessly from 
weak through moderate to strong inversion. 
• 
Compactness: Built on a small, physically meaningful set of parameters, making it computationally 
efficient and well-suited for low-power analog and RF circuit simulation. 
6. Summary Comparison: Hand Calculation vs. EDA Simulation 
• 
Hand Calculations: Limited to Level-1 formulations (~7 parameters) to evaluate basic circuit operating 
points and first-order functional behavior. 
• 
EDA Simulations: Utilize higher-level models (e.g., Level 4 / BSIM with over 100 parameters) to 
accurately simulate short-channel physics, leakage, parasitic interactions, and fabrication tolerances prior 
to silicon commitment.
