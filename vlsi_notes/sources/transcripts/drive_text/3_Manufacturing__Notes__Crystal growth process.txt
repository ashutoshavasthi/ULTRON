Czochralski (CZ) Technique for Single Silicon Crystal Growth
These notes summarize the process of drawing a single silicon crystal from polysilicon using the Czochralski
(CZ) technique, which is a key process in VLSI manufacturing.
I. Introduction and Raw Material
1. Purpose: The Czochralski technique (CZ) is one of two primary mechanisms (the other being the Float
Zone method) used to draw a single silicon crystal from raw polysilicon.
2. Raw Material: The raw material is Polysilicon, which is Electronic Grade Silicon (EGS). This EGS is
ultra-pure, reaching approximately 99.999999999% purity.
3. Preparation: The polysilicon rod is broken or crushed into small pieces. These pieces are referred to as
the charge,.
4. Alternate Name: The CZ technique is also known as the Liquid Solid Mono Component Growth
System.
5. Historical Note: This technique was invented in 1918.

III. Working Mechanism
The process converts the solid polysilicon charge into a single-crystal ingot.
1. Preparation: The charge is placed in the crucible, the chamber is evacuated, and then Argon gas is
introduced to set the working pressure,.
2. Melting: The graphite heater raises the temperature of the charge up to the melting point of silicon,
which is around 
,. The solid charge converts into the molten, liquid phase.
3. Seeding: The pulling shaft lowers the solid seed crystal down until it just touches the surface of the
molten silicon and begins to melt slowly,.
4. Growth (Pulling): As soon as the seed crystal starts melting, the shaft slowly begins to pull the crystal
upward,.
5. Solidification: Molten silicon sticks to the surface of the seed crystal. As the seed is pulled up, the
silicon cools down rapidly and solidifies progressively at the solid-liquid interface, forming the single
crystal,.
IV. Key Process Controls
Two factors are critical for determining the quality and shape of the resulting crystal:
1. Rotation: The crucible and the pulling shaft rotate in opposite directions (counter-rotation). This
maintains the molten silicon at a uniform temperature throughout the crucible, preventing the
formation of local hot or cold regions,.
2. Pulling Rate: The rate at which the seed crystal is withdrawn must be strictly maintained.
The pulling rate dictates the diameter of the single silicon crystal that grows,.
A typical pulling rate is a few millimeters per minute (
),.
If the pulling rate is too slow, the seed crystal may completely melt down, preventing crystal
growth.
3. Orientation Template: The crystal that grows automatically takes on the same crystal orientation as
the seed crystal used (e.g., if the seed is 111, the resulting crystal will be 111),.
V. Outcomes and Disadvantages
1. Product: The resulting cylindrical single silicon crystal is called an ingot.
2. Advantage of CZ: The CZ technique is popular because it is capable of growing large diameter single
silicon crystals. Typical diameters range from 8 to 16 inches.
