Density of States 
 
1. Review: 
Concept 
Description 
Fermi Function f(E) 
Represents the probability of finding an electron at a specific 
energy (E). 
Fermi Level (E_F) 
A statistical construct where the probability of finding an electron 
is 50% at any temperature. The position of E_F dictates whether a 
material is n-type (upper half of band gap) or p-type (lower half of 
band gap). 
Effective Mass (m*) 
Summarizes the effects of the crystal structure and quantum 
mechanics on electron transport, allowing classical equations to be 
applied. Both electrons and holes have their own effective mass 
(m*electron or m*hole). 
Hole 
A vacancy left behind when an electron moves from the valence 
band to the conduction band. It is modeled as a quasi-particle with 
a positive charge that moves in the direction of the applied field. 
2. Density of States (DOS), g(E) 
Density of States is a crucial concept, as the number of electrons or holes in a semiconductor 
depends on both the Fermi function (probability) and the Density of States (availability). 
A. Definition 
Density of States (g(E)) is defined as: The number of available energy states per unit energy 
per unit volume. 
B. Significance 
To find the total number of electrons (n) or holes (p)—which is necessary to calculate 
current density and understand device operation—one must know the Density of States. 
C. Units 
In semiconductor physics, calculations often use CGS units (centimeter, not meter). 
The standard unit for Density of States is: states per electron volt per centimeter cube (per 
eV per cm^3).

4. Graphical Representation of DOS 
If Density of States (g(E)) is plotted against energy (E): 
1. Parabolic Dependence: g(E) goes as the square root of E (or E-Ec or Ev-E). As energy 
increases, the number of available states also increases. 

--- page 3 ---
2. Starting Point (Ec): For electrons, DOS only begins at the bottom of the conduction band 
(Ec). Below Ec (i.e., in the band gap), there are no states. 
3. Hole DOS: Hole DOS starts from the top of the valence band (Ev) and increases as energy 
moves downward. 
The relationship results in parabolic-like humps starting at Ec and Ev.

band.

conduction band and holes in the valence band, even in pure (intrinsic) silicon at room 
temperature (300 K) without any external stimulus. This explains how electrons can jump

7. Intrinsic Semiconductors 
An intrinsic semiconductor is a pure crystal with no added external impurities. For an 
intrinsic semiconductor in equilibrium, with no external fields or light applied, the electron 
concentration (n) must be exactly equal to the hole concentration (p).
