--- slide 3 ---
Epitaxy
Epitaxial and polysilicon film growth
➢
Deposits a monocrystalline film.
➢
Greek roots: epi means “above”, and taxis
means “ordered”.
➢
Uses gaseous or liquid precursors.
➢
The substrate seeds the film’s crystal structure.
➢
Temperature and substrate determine the
physical structure of the deposited film.
--- slide 4 ---
Types of Epitaxy
Homoepitaxy:
➢
Grow films purer than the substrate.
➢
Create layers with different doping levels.
Heteroepitaxy:
➢
Integrate crystalline layers of
different materials.
➢
Engineer band structures for electronic
and optoelectronic devices.
--- slide 5 ---
Advantages of Epitaxy
Applications
➢
High-purity, monocrystalline silicon in the
device region.
➢
Lower temperatures than implantation and diffusion
allow sharper doping profiles.
➢
A wide range of thicknesses and doping profiles,
with opportunities for band-gap engineering.
➢
Suitable for engineered semiconductor structures:
▪  HBTs: heterojunction bipolar transistors
   (H. Kroemer, Nobel Prize 2000)
▪  HEMTs: high-electron-mobility transistors
   based on a two-dimensional electron gas (2DEG)
Engineered wafers
– Clean, flat layers on less ideal Si substrates
– Layers on SOI structures
– Silicon on sapphire
– Higher-purity layers on lower-quality SiC
CMOS structures
– Layers with different doping
– A p layer on p⁺ substrate to reduce latch-up
Bipolar transistors
Epitaxy enables buried-layer structures.
III–V devices
Interface quality matters for HBTs, LEDs
and lasers.

Vapor Phase Epitaxy
Steps involved in a CVD process
➢
Crystallization from the vapor phase.
➢
Uses chemical vapor deposition.
➢
Controls thickness, doping and crystallinity.
➢
High-temperature epitaxy: 800–1100 °C.
➢
Offers flexibility in device fabrication.
Chemical Vapor Deposition
1.
Reactants reach the deposition region.
2.
Reactants cross the boundary layer from the
main gas stream to the wafer surface.
3.
Reactants adsorb on the wafer.
4.
Surface reactions include decomposition,
chemical reaction, migration to kinks and
ledges, incorporation, emission and
redeposition.
5.
Byproducts desorb from the surface.
6.
Byproducts cross the boundary layer.
7.
Byproducts leave the deposition region.
Steps 2–5 are most important for growth rate.
Steps 3–5 form the “surface reaction” processes.
