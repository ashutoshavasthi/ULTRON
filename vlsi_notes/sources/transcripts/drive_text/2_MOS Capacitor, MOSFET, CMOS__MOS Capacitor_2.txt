MOS Capacitance-Voltage (C-V) Characteristics
I. Introduction to the MOS Capacitor
The MOS capacitor is a fundamental structure essential for understanding transistors like the MOSFET
(Metal-Oxide-Semiconductor Field-Effect Transistor).
Structure: The device consists of three layers:
1. Metal: The gate terminal where voltage (
) is applied.
2. Oxide: A layer of insulator (dielectric).
3. Semiconductor: The substrate (we use p-type silicon for our discussion).
Principle: The voltage applied to the gate (
) controls how the energy bands in the semiconductor bend,
which, in turn, changes the charge distribution near the interface, leading to different operating conditions.
II. Operating Conditions in P-Type Silicon
In a p-type semiconductor, the majority carriers are holes (positive charges). The gate voltage (
)
determines the concentration and type of carriers at the oxide-semiconductor interface.

A. Accumulation
1. When a negative voltage is applied to the metal gate, it strongly attracts the holes from the p-type
silicon bulk toward the interface.
2. A high density of holes forms a very thin layer right at the interface. This layer is called the
accumulation layer.
B. Depletion
1. When a small positive voltage is applied, the positive potential repels the mobile holes away from the
interface.
2. This pushing away of holes creates a region near the interface called the depletion region.
3. In this region, the holes (mobile positive carriers) are gone, leaving behind the immobile, negatively
charged ionized acceptor impurities.
