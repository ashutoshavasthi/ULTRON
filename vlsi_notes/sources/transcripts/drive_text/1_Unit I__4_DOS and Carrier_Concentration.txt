Density of States and Intrinsic Carrier Concentration 
I. Introduction 
A. Defining Intrinsic Semiconductors 
An intrinsic semiconductor is pristine pure, meaning nothing has been added, and it is in 
equilibrium. In an intrinsic semiconductor, the number of electrons and the number of holes 
must be exactly the same. This equal concentration is known as the intrinsic carrier 
concentration (nᵢ), measured per volume (e.g., per centimeter cube). 
B. The Role of Density of States (DOS) 
To find the total number of electrons or holes in a semiconductor, two main factors must be 
considered: 
1. Probability: The Fermi function provides the probability of finding electrons or holes at a 
specific energy level. 
2. Available States: The density of states (DOS) tells you how many states are there for the 
electrons to occupy or for the holes to occupy. 
The total number of electrons or holes is always calculated by integrating the product of the 
probability (Fermi function) and the density of states (available states) over the entire 
energy landscape.

simplified into an exponential function (Boltzmann approximation) under conditions of

V. Intrinsic Carrier Concentration (n_i) 
In an intrinsic semiconductor, the electron concentration (N) equals the hole concentration 
(P), both equal to the intrinsic carrier concentration (nᵢ).

• Dependence on DOS: nᵢ depends on √(NCNV). 
• Temperature and Band Gap Dependence: Strong dependence on band gap (EG) and T. 
• Physical Meaning: nᵢ represents the minimum concentration of electrons and holes that
