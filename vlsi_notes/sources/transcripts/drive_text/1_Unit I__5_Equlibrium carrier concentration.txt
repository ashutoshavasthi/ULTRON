I. Fundamental Concepts 
1. Prerequisites (From previous discussions) 
• Fermi-Dirac statistics: Describes the probability of finding an electron. 
• Density of States g(E): Represents how many energy states are available for electrons to 
occupy. 
• Carrier Concentration: The product of the density of states and the Fermi function 
determines the number of electrons or holes present. 
2. Intrinsic Semiconductors (Pure Material) 
• An intrinsic semiconductor is pristine and pure, containing no impurities. 
• In an intrinsic semiconductor, electrons (n) and holes (p) are generated in equal numbers 
solely due to thermal energy. 
• The intrinsic carrier concentration is denoted by nᵢ. 
• Condition for intrinsic material: n = p = nᵢ. 
• Example: For silicon, nᵢ ≈ 1.5 × 10^10 cm⁻³ at room temperature. 
 
3. Extrinsic Semiconductors (Doping) 
• Doping involves adding external dopants to tune the conductivity and change the number 
of available carriers. 
• N-Type Doping (Donors, N_D): 
   ◦ Impurities like Phosphorous or Arsenic are added. 
   ◦ These atoms donate an extra electron. 
   ◦ The electron concentration (n) is higher than the hole concentration (p). Electrons are 
the majority carriers; holes are the minority carriers. 

--- page 2 ---
   ◦ The Fermi level (E_F) shifts into the top half of the band gap, closer to the conduction 
band (E_C). 
• P-Type Doping (Acceptors, N_A): 
   ◦ Impurities like Boron are added. 
   ◦ These atoms accept an electron, effectively creating a hole. 
   ◦ The hole concentration (p) is higher than the electron concentration (n). Holes are the 
majority carriers; electrons are the minority carriers. 
   ◦ The Fermi level (E_F) shifts into the bottom half of the band gap, closer to the valence 
band (E_V). 
4. Role of Intrinsic Concentration (nᵢ) and Band Gap (E_g) 
• nᵢ is the minimum (background or default) carrier concentration always present in a 
material due to thermal generation; it cannot be decreased below this value. 
• nᵢ depends exponentially on the band gap (E_g) and temperature. 
• Impact of Band Gap: 
   ◦ Lower E_g (e.g., Germanium, 0.68 eV) leads to a higher nᵢ. 
   ◦ Higher E_g (e.g., Gallium Nitride, 3.4 eV) leads to a much lower nᵢ. 
• Technological Implication (Leakage Current/Noise): Materials with higher nᵢ (smaller E_g) 
inherently lead to higher background current which manifests as increased leakage current 
or noise in devices like transistors or photo detectors.

III. Conceptual Insights for Energy Bands in Equilibrium 
1. The Fermi Level (E_F) 
• E_F is a statistical construct; it is not a real energy level with carriers. 
• In equilibrium, the Fermi level is flat (straight) across the material. 
• A slope in E_F indicates non-equilibrium (current flow). 
 
2. Forbidden Gap 
• The region between E_C and E_V is the forbidden gap. 
• Carriers cannot exist in the forbidden gap because g(E)=0 there. 
• Even if the Fermi function gives a 50% probability i.e., f(E)=0.5 at E_F, the concentration is 0 
as n= g(E)·f(E)=0.

3. Conduction and Valence Bands (E_C and E_V) 
• E_C and E_V represent electron energy, linked to potential. 
• Flat Bands (No Slope): If Ec and Ev are flat (straight) with respect to position (x), it means 
there is no electric field (E=0) in the material  
• Band Bending: A slope in E_C or E_V means there is an electric field is present. 
4. Equilibrium under Non-Uniform Doping 
When a semiconductor has a gradual change in doping concentration (e.g., N_D increasing 
left→right) while remaining in equilibrium: 
1. E_F remains flat (no current flows). 
2. The conduction band and valence band must bend (have a slope) to maintain the required 
constant band gap and the local Ec - Ef spacing. 
3. This bending (slope in Ec and Ev) generates an internal electric field. 
 
4. This internal electric field balances the tendency of carriers to diffuse (move from high 
concentration to low concentration) due to the doping gradient, thereby ensuring zero net 
current (equilibrium)
