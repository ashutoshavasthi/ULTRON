Thermal Oxidation of Silicon
I. Fundamentals of Thermal Oxidation
1. What is Thermal Oxidation?
It is a process where a silicon (Si) substrate reacts with an oxygen-containing gas in a high-
temperature environment.
The reaction produces silicon dioxide (
).
The basic reaction involves the oxygen or water vapor flowing into high-temperature furnaces
reacting with the silicon of the substrate.
2. Why is Silicon Dioxide Important?
The silicon/silicon dioxide interface is one of the best electrical interfaces possible, which is a main
reason why silicon is the dominant semiconductor used today.
 films are excellent electrical insulators.
They have a very large band gap of 9 electron volts (eV), compared to 1.1 eV in silicon.
These oxides have excellent electrical properties, including a very high breakdown field.
3. Applications of Thermally Grown Oxides
Gate Dielectrics: Thin oxides are used as gate oxides or gate dielectrics in MOSFETs.
Isolation: Thicker oxides are used as field oxides or isolation oxides between neighboring transistors.
A thick isolation oxide prevents parasitic leakage paths by increasing the threshold voltage in
those regions.
Dopant Masking: Oxides can be made thick enough to block the introduction of dopants (like
phosphorus or boron) during thermal diffusion or ion implantation.
II. The Oxidation Environment and Procedure
Equipment: Oxides are typically grown in oxidation diffusion furnaces, which contain quartz tubes
surrounded by heating coils.
Furnaces can be horizontal or vertical; vertical furnaces have a smaller footprint in the clean
room and offer more symmetric gas flow around the wafers.
Procedure:
1. A boat holding 50 to 100 wafers, stacked closely, is loaded very slowly into the furnace to
minimize thermal shock.
2. The cap is closed, and inert gases are flowed in to flush out the air.
3. The temperature is raised to the operating level, typically around 
 to 
.
4. Oxidation proceeds, and then the temperature is ramped down and the wafers are slowly pulled
out.
Temperature Sensitivity: Maintaining uniform temperature is critical because oxidation rates are
exponentially dependent on temperature (Arrhenius dependence).
